The Story

There are stories buried beneath the stories we have been told. Ancient names carved into stone. Forgotten civilizations. Sacred traditions. Lost languages.

The Awakening is an original fictional journey inspired by humanity's ancient world. It follows Tacoreon as he searches through forgotten ruins, ancient memories and mysterious texts.

The deeper he travels, the more he realizes that the greatest mystery may not be what humanity forgot— but why.

Chapter I

The Forgotten Voice

The first thing he heard was the wind.

It moved across the empty landscape like a voice that had traveled thousands of years to find him.

Beneath his feet was dust. Beneath the dust were stones. And beneath the stones was a world civilization had almost forgotten.

He stood alone beneath an enormous night sky, staring toward the distant ruins.

"Every civilization leaves a memory behind. The question is whether anyone will listen."

He had spent years searching for answers. Not answers that could be found in a textbook, but answers hidden between ancient stories.

Then he found the tablet.

He reached toward it.

And the moment his fingers touched the stone, everything changed.

Chapter II

Before the Beginning

Before kings, before temples, before the great cities, people looked toward the heavens and asked the same questions humanity still asks today.

Where did we come from?

Why are we here?

And what existed before the beginning?

The oldest surviving civilizations recorded their answers on clay, stone and parchment.

Their stories spoke of creation, humanity, order and chaos.

The stories were not simply about gods. They were also about humanity.
Illustration II — Before the Beginning

Illustrated by Tacoreon Holmes

Chapter III

The Mother of Life

Among the oldest traditions was the memory of a mother.

A creator. A giver of life. A figure remembered under different names.

In ancient Mesopotamian traditions, names such as Nintur, Ninmah and Aruru appear in different literary contexts.

The stories changed over centuries. But the memory of creation remained.

Life came from something greater than the individual.

Illustrated by Tacoreon Holmes

Chapter IV

The Garden

Every culture seemed to remember a place where humanity existed close to the divine.

A garden.

A sacred land.

A place where knowledge and life stood beside one another.

The Hebrew tradition preserved the story of Eden.

Other ancient traditions preserved their own sacred landscapes.

Illustration IV — The Garden

Illustrated by Tacoreon Holmes

Perhaps the garden was more than a place.

Perhaps it represented humanity's memory of innocence.

Chapter V

The Watchers

Night fell quickly.

He looked toward the stars.

Ancient people had watched those same stars for thousands of years.

They mapped them. Named them. Built monuments beneath them. And told stories about beings who descended from the heavens.

"Humanity has always looked upward."
Illustration V — The Watchers

Illustrated by Tacoreon Holmes

He closed his eyes.

The voice returned.

This time, he understood one word.

Remember.

Chapter VI

The Awakening

Dawn arrived slowly.

The darkness disappeared from the horizon.

He looked down at the tablet.

The symbols had not changed. But now he knew what they meant.

"Who will remember when everyone else has forgotten?"

For the first time, he understood that awakening was not about discovering an ancient secret.

It was about discovering himself.

Illustration VI — The Awakening

Illustrated by Tacoreon Holmes

He stepped forward.

The journey had only begun.

Chapter VII

The Door Beneath the Earth

The second sun had not yet risen.

Tacoreon stood at the edge of the ruins, staring at an entrance he had never noticed before.

It was beneath the broken temple.

A narrow staircase descended into darkness.

For thousands of years, sand had covered it.

Until now.

He took the first step.

Then another.

At the bottom was a stone doorway.

Across it were three symbols: a circle, a river and an eye.

Tacoreon raised the tablet.

The symbols matched.

The stone door moved.

Illustration VII — The Door Beneath the Earth

Illustrated by Tacoreon Holmes

Chapter VIII

The Chamber

The walls were covered with images.

People. Stars. Animals. Ships. Mountains.

And enormous figures standing beside human beings.

At the center stood a stone table.

Upon it was another tablet.

Tacoreon reached toward it.

"You have remembered."

At the far end stood a massive carving of a woman holding a child.

Beneath the carving was a name.

NINTUR.
Illustration VIII — The Chamber

Illustrated by Tacoreon Holmes

Chapter IX

The Message

Tacoreon read the inscription slowly.

It spoke of a time when humanity would forget where it came from.

The final words were addressed directly to whoever discovered the chamber.

REMEMBER THE HUMANITY WITHIN THE STORY.

The chamber wasn't asking him to worship the past.

It was asking him to listen.

To compare. To question. To learn.

Illustration IX — The Ancient Message

Illustrated by Tacoreon Holmes

Chapter X

The First Memory

The old man walked toward the stone table.

"You've been searching for answers," he said.

Tacoreon nodded.

"For years."

"And what if the answers aren't what you expect?"

"Then I'll still want to know."

The old man smiled.

"That is why you're here."

The ancient city appeared before them.

A river flowed through green fields. People walked between mud-brick houses. Children played. Farmers worked.

It was alive.

Not a ruin.

A civilization.

"We were human before we became history."
Illustration X — The First Memory

Illustrated by Tacoreon Holmes

Chapter XI

The River of Memory

The river moved silently through the ancient city.

Tacoreon watched the people gather along its banks.

Thousands of objects rested beneath the water.

Thousands of memories.

Thousands of voices.

"They are leaving memories for those who come after them."

Tacoreon looked at the ancient city.

They had not simply been abandoned.

They had been waiting.

Illustration XI — The River of Memory

Illustrated by Tacoreon Holmes

Chapter XII

The City of Beginnings

The vision returned with the sound of water.

Tacoreon stood beside the ancient river, watching the first light spread across the city.

Towers rose above the houses. Temples stood at the center. Smoke drifted into the morning sky.

"A beginning."

"Whose beginning?"

The old man looked toward the city.

"Humanity's memory of itself."
Illustration XII — The City of Beginnings

Illustrated by Tacoreon Holmes

Chapter XIII

The Scribe

The scribe led Tacoreon into a room behind the temple.

Hundreds of clay tablets covered the walls.

There were records of births, deaths, trade, prayers, stories, laws and songs.

Then Tacoreon found something different.

A tablet containing a drawing of a woman holding a child.

NINTUR.

"The memory of the mother," the scribe said.

Illustration XIII — The Scribe's Archive

Chapter XIV

The Choice

The vision began to disappear.

The buildings faded. The river became transparent. The people vanished into light.

Tacoreon returned to the underground chamber.

Two doors stood before him.

One led upward.

The other descended deeper into the earth.

"The part of the story nobody remembers."

Tacoreon stared into the darkness.

He stepped forward.

Illustration XIV — The Choice

Illustrated by Tacoreon Holmes

And descended.

Chapter XV

Beneath the First Temple

The staircase seemed to have no end.

Tacoreon descended deeper beneath the ancient city. The air grew colder. The walls became older.

The stonework changed.

These stones were not like the temple above. They belonged to something much older.

At the bottom he discovered a circular chamber.

In the center was a pool of perfectly still water.

The old man stopped at the edge.

"This is where the memory begins."

Tacoreon looked into the water.

He did not see his reflection.

He saw a city.

A city older than the ruins above it.

And somewhere inside that city, someone was waiting.

Illustration XV — Beneath the First Temple

Illustrated by Tacoreon Holmes

The water began to glow.

Then a voice spoke from beneath the surface.

"Tacoreon..."

He froze.

The voice knew his name.

And the awakening had entered its next stage.
