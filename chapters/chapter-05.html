CHAPTER FIVE

JERICHO

✦ ✦ ✦

They left before sunrise.

Maya sat beside Elias in the front of the vehicle while David drove.

For the first hour, none of them spoke.

The tablet was secured inside a protective case beneath Maya’s seat.

The seal was wrapped separately.

Elias’s father’s notebook rested inside his satchel.

Three objects.

Three pieces of a mystery that had survived generations.

Maya finally broke the silence.

“Why Jericho?”

Elias looked through the window.

“Because that’s where my father found the first symbol.”

“You said he was ordered to forget it.”

“Yes.”

“By whom?”

Elias was quiet.

“An organization.”

Maya waited.

“What organization?”

Elias looked at her.

“They called themselves the Keepers.”

David glanced at him through the rearview mirror.

“Keepers of what?”

Elias looked down at the notebook.

“That was never explained.”

Maya studied him.

“You’ve known about them all these years?”

“I knew my father feared them.”

“That’s different.”

“I know.”

The road continued through the desert.

Eventually, the landscape began to change.

The ancient hills appeared in the distance.

Jericho.

One of the oldest continuously inhabited places on Earth.

Maya had studied its history for years.

But now she was seeing it differently.

Not as an archaeological site.

As a clue.

They arrived at an old excavation area shortly after noon.

Elias stepped out.

He stood perfectly still.

Maya noticed the expression on his face.

“You’ve been here before.”

Elias nodded.

“Once.”

“When?”

“When I was seventeen.”

David looked at him.

“You came here with your father?”

“Yes.”

“What happened?”

Elias stared toward the hills.

“My father brought me here because he wanted me to see something.”

“What?”

Elias walked toward the ruins.

“The doorway.”

Maya followed.

“There was actually a doorway?”

“Not like you think.”

They reached a section of exposed stone.

Elias knelt beside a partially buried wall.

He brushed away the dust.

A symbol appeared.

Maya froze.

It was the same symbol carved into the seal.

She looked at Elias.

“You knew it was here.”

“I suspected.”

“You brought us here because of it.”

“Yes.”

David crouched beside them.

“There are no records of this structure.”

“That’s because the structure isn’t part of the official excavation.”

Maya stared at him.

“Then what is it?”

Elias brushed more dirt away.

A narrow opening appeared beneath the stones.

Maya’s pulse quickened.

“There.”

Elias nodded.

“The doorway.”

David looked at the dark opening.

“Where does it go?”

Elias took a flashlight from his bag.

“My father never found out.”

Maya looked at him.

“How do you know?”

Elias turned toward her.

“Because he told me never to open it.”

The three of them stood in silence.

Then Maya looked at the tablet.

The ancient markings seemed almost visible through the protective case.

She remembered Elias’s words.

A memory.

A doorway.

A seal.

And a name.

Yehoshua.

Maya stepped toward the opening.

Elias grabbed her arm.

“Wait.”

She turned.

“What?”

He pointed toward the stone.

There were words carved beneath the symbol.

Maya leaned closer.

For several moments, neither of them spoke.

Then Elias translated the first line.

“Those who enter…”

He stopped.

Maya looked at him.

“What does the rest say?”

Elias swallowed.

“Will remember.”

The wind moved across the ruins.

Maya looked into the darkness.

Then, from somewhere beneath the earth—

came three slow knocks.

David stepped backward.

“Tell me that was the wind.”

Elias shook his head.

“It wasn’t.”

Three more knocks answered from below.

Maya looked at the doorway.

Something was on the other side.

And it knew they had arrived.
