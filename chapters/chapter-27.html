CHAPTER XXXVI

THE NAME BEFORE NAMES

The first sun rose over a world that no longer existed.

Maya watched it break across the horizon.

For a moment, there was peace.

Then the sun stopped.

Not setting.

Not rising.

Stopped.

Elias looked upward.

“That shouldn’t happen.”

Maya turned toward him.

“What is happening?”

Her mother answered.

“The world is waiting.”

“For what?”

Her mother looked at Maya.

“For your choice.”

The other Maya stepped toward the black stone.

“Come here.”

Maya didn’t move.

“What happens if I touch it?”

“You remember everything.”

Elias shook his head.

“No.”

Maya looked at him.

“Why?”

“Because some memories don’t belong to you.”

The other Maya smiled.

“They belong to all of us.”

Maya stepped forward.

Her fingers touched the black stone.

The world disappeared.

She saw the first people.

Not kings.

Not gods.

Not monsters.

Just people.

They looked toward the sky and wondered why they existed.

Then Maya saw something descend from the darkness.

Not a creature.

A presence.

A consciousness.

It entered the black stone.

And the first people heard its voice.

“Remember.”

They began recording everything.

Their discoveries.

Their wars.

Their deaths.

Their children.

Their mistakes.

They created the Keepers to preserve the memory.

But eventually the Keepers became afraid.

They believed memory itself was dangerous.

So they created a seal.

A seal that would erase the world whenever humanity reached a certain point.

Maya watched civilization after civilization disappear.

Again.

Again.

Again.

And every time—

someone remembered.

Her.

Maya.

The first memory.

The one who carried everything forward.

The vision changed.

She saw herself standing before the first Keeper.

He was young then.

He held a blade.

“You must forget,” he told her.

Maya answered:

“If I forget, who will remember?”

The Keeper lowered the blade.

“We will.”

Maya shook her head.

“No.”

She pointed toward the black stone.

“You will eventually become afraid.”

The Keeper whispered:

“Then what do you propose?”

Maya looked at the sky.

“I will become the memory.”

The vision shattered.

Maya fell backward.

Elias caught her.

Her mother knelt beside her.

“What did you see?”

Maya looked at them.

Her voice was different now.

Calm.

Certain.

“I remember.”

The other Maya stood across from her.

“What do you remember?”

Maya looked directly at her.

“Everything.”

The other Maya smiled.

Then her body began dissolving into light.

Maya reached for her.

“Wait!”

The woman shook her head.

“You don’t need me anymore.”

“Who are you?”

The woman smiled one final time.

“You.”

Then she vanished.

The black stone went dark.

Maya looked toward Elias.

“What happens now?”

He stared at the horizon.

“Now they come.”

“Who?”

A sound echoed across the ancient world.

Thousands of voices.

The Shomrim.

But beneath them was another voice.

One Maya recognized.

Her own.

“The cycle ends with you.”

Maya stood.

The first sun began moving again.

And somewhere beyond the horizon—

the world began to wake.
