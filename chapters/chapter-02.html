Chapter Two

The Professor

Professor Elias Ben-Ami arrived before sunrise.

Maya had expected an older man carrying a briefcase filled with books.

Instead, a dark vehicle stopped outside the excavation compound and Elias stepped out carrying only a worn leather satchel.

He looked toward the excavation.

Then toward Maya.

For several seconds neither of them spoke.

“You said the name was Yehoshua,” he finally said.

Maya nodded.

“I did.”

Elias walked toward her.

“Show me.”

Maya led him into the examination tent.

The tablet rested beneath a glass cover.

Elias stopped when he saw it.

The expression on his face changed.

Not surprise.

Recognition.

Maya noticed immediately.

“You’ve seen it before.”

Elias didn’t answer.

He moved closer.

✦ ✦ ✦

His eyes followed the markings across the clay.

“Where did you get this?”

“Yesterday.”

“I mean where?”

Maya gave him the excavation coordinates.

Elias stared at her.

“That isn’t possible.”

“That’s what I said.”

He removed his glasses.

“Who else has touched it?”

“David and me.”

“Anyone else?”

“No.”

“Good.”

Maya frowned.

“Why?”

“Because until we understand what this is, nobody else should.”

David entered the tent.

“You guys want coffee?”

Neither answered.

David looked between them.

“What?”

Elias pointed toward the tablet.

“Have you ever seen anything like this?”

David shook his head.

“I’m an archaeologist, Professor. I’ve seen a lot of clay.”

Elias smiled faintly.

“This isn’t about the clay.”

He leaned closer.

“It’s about the language.”

Maya folded her arms.

“You recognize it?”

“Parts of it.”

“Cuneiform?”

“Some of it.”

“And the rest?”

Elias looked at Maya.

“That is what concerns me.”

He carefully examined the tablet without touching it.

“These symbols appear to have been written by someone familiar with several ancient writing systems.”

Maya stepped closer.

“Mesopotamian?”

“Partly.”

“Hebrew?”

“Not exactly.”

“Samaritan?”

Elias paused.

“Closer.”

Maya’s eyes narrowed.

“Then what is it?”

✦ ✦ ✦

“I don’t know.”

That answer unsettled her more than anything else.

Elias was famous for knowing.

He had spent forty years studying languages that had disappeared thousands of years ago.

Yet he was staring at a tablet as though it had violated everything he understood about history.

Maya pointed toward the rear of the tablet.

“You said you recognized the name.”

Elias nodded.

“Yehoshua.”

“What does it mean?”

Elias looked at her.

“It is an ancient form of a name you already know.”

“Jesus?”

Elias didn’t immediately answer.

“Yes.”

The tent became silent.

David looked confused.

“Jesus?”

Elias nodded.

“But this tablet predates the first century by centuries.”

Maya stared at him.

“Then how can his name be here?”

Elias looked down at the tablet.

“That is the question.”

✦ ✦ ✦

He finally opened his leather satchel.

Inside were photographs, handwritten notes, and several old reproductions of ancient inscriptions.

He placed three photographs on the table.

Maya recognized them immediately.

The first was from a museum collection in Baghdad.

The second came from an excavation in Israel.

The third was from a private archive in Europe.

Different places.

Different discoveries.

The same symbols.

Maya looked at Elias.

“You knew about these.”

“I’ve been studying them for twenty-seven years.”

“Why didn’t anyone publish this?”

Elias looked toward the entrance of the tent.

“Because every scholar who gets close to this question eventually discovers that the evidence doesn’t fit the accepted timeline.”

Maya looked back at the tablet.

“What timeline?”

Elias lowered his voice.

“The beginning.”

A cold feeling moved through Maya.

“The beginning of what?”

Elias looked directly at her.

“Everything we think we know about humanity.”

✦ ✦ ✦

He placed one finger beside the inscription.

“This isn’t a record of a king.”

“Then what is it?”

Elias studied the ancient marks.

“A memory.”

Maya stared at him.

“A memory of what?”

Elias didn’t answer.

Outside, the first light of morning appeared over the desert.

And beneath that light, something buried for thousands of years had begun to speak again.
