CHAPTER TWO

THE PROFESSOR

Professor Elias Ben-Ami arrived before sunrise.

Maya had expected an older man carrying a briefcase filled with books.

Instead, a dark vehicle stopped outside the excavation compound and Elias stepped out carrying only a worn leather satchel.

He looked toward the excavation.

Then toward Maya.

For several seconds neither of them spoke.

“You said the name was Yehoshua,” he finally said.

Maya nodded.

“I did.”

Elias walked toward her.

“Show me.”

Maya led him into the examination tent.

The tablet rested beneath a glass cover.

Elias stopped when he saw it.

The expression on his face changed.

Not surprise.

Recognition.

Maya noticed immediately.

“You’ve seen it before.”

Elias didn’t answer.

He moved closer.

His eyes followed the markings across the clay.

“Where did you get this?”

“Yesterday.”

“I mean where?”

Maya gave him the excavation coordinates.

Elias stared at her.

“That isn’t possible.”

“That’s what I said.”

He removed his glasses.

“Who else has touched it?”

“David and me.”

“Anyone else?”

“No.”

“Good.”

Maya frowned.

“Why?”

“Because until we understand what this is, nobody else should.”

David entered the tent.

“You guys want coffee?”

Neither answered.

David looked between them.

“What?”

Elias pointed toward the tablet.

“Have you ever seen anything like this?”

David shook his head.

“I’m an archaeologist, Professor. I’ve seen a lot of clay.”

Elias smiled faintly.

“This isn’t about the clay.”

He leaned closer.

“It’s about the language.”

Maya folded her arms.

“You recognize it?”

“Parts of it.”

“Cuneiform?”

“Some of it.”

“And the rest?”

Elias looked at Maya.

“That is what concerns me.”

He carefully examined the tablet without touching it.

“These symbols appear to have been written by someone familiar with several ancient writing systems.”

Maya stepped closer.

“Mesopotamian?”

“Partly.”

“Hebrew?”

“Not exactly.”

“Samaritan?”

Elias paused.

“Closer.”

Maya’s eyes narrowed.

“Then what is it?”

Elias looked at the tablet again.

Neither of them spoke.
