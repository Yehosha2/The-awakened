XXXVIII CHAPTER 38 The Difference The sky was falling. Not metaphorically. Stars tore loose from the darkness and descended like burning fragments. Maya stood beneath them. She wasn’t afraid. Not anymore. Elias grabbed her hand. “We need to get out of here.” “There is nowhere to go.” “There has to be.” Maya looked at him. “There isn’t.” Her mother stepped forward. “There is one place.” Maya turned. “Where?” Her mother pointed toward the door in the sky. “Beyond it.” Elias shook his head. “No.” Maya stared at the enormous doorway. “What is beyond the door?” Her mother whispered: “Everything that was forgotten.” The first Keeper approached. His staff was broken. The symbols across his robes were fading. “If she enters, the boundary will collapse.” Maya looked at him. “Then why was the door created?” The old man lowered his eyes. “For you.” Maya froze. “What?” “The door wasn’t built to keep something out.” He looked toward the sky. “It was built to let you return.” Elias stepped forward. “Return from where?” The Keeper looked at Maya. “From the place before this world.” The ground shook. The doorway began descending. Closer. Closer. Until it hung above Maya like a second moon. A voice came through. “Māyā.” The sound made every stone around them vibrate. Maya closed her eyes. Memories flooded through her. She remembered the first world. The second. The third. The countless worlds that had come and gone. She remembered every time humanity had forgotten. Every time the Shomrim had rebuilt the seal. Every time she had returned. And she remembered the first choice. She had created the cycle. Not to punish humanity. To protect it. But somewhere along the way, the Keepers had misunderstood. They believed memory was the danger. Maya finally understood. Forgetting was the danger. She opened her eyes. “The cycle doesn’t need to continue.” Her mother took her hand. “What will you do?” Maya looked at Elias. He already knew. “No,” he whispered. She smiled sadly. “I have to.” “You don’t.” “If I don’t go through, the door will keep opening.” “Then close it.” “I can’t.” “Why?” “Because it was never mine to close.” The doorway opened completely. A vast light appeared beyond it. Maya stepped forward. Elias followed. She stopped him. “You can’t come.” “I’ve followed you this far.” “I know.” “Then let me finish it with you.” Maya touched his face. “You already did.” Her mother began crying. “Maya…” Maya turned toward her. “I finally understand why you gave me away.” Her mother shook her head. “I was trying to save you.” “You did.” Maya kissed her forehead. Then she turned toward the door. Elias reached for her. “Maya!” She looked back. For a moment, she was simply herself. Not the first memory. Not the chosen one. Not the keeper of worlds. Just Maya. She smiled. “Remember me.” Then she stepped through. The doorway vanished. The falling stars stopped. The world became silent. Elias stood alone. Her mother closed her eyes. Then— a single star appeared above them. It began moving. Forming a shape. A symbol. Maya’s symbol. Elias stared at it. “She didn’t disappear.” The first Keeper whispered: “No.” He looked toward the sky. “She went home.” And somewhere beyond the stars— Maya opened her eyes.
