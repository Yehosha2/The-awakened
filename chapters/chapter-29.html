CHAPTER XXIX

THE LAST KEEPER

Darkness swallowed the mountain.

For several seconds, Maya heard nothing.

Then—

a heartbeat.

Boom.

Another.

Boom.

She realized it wasn’t hers.

Something was standing behind her.

Maya slowly turned.

The creature was gone.

So was the gate.

Only Elias remained.

He was on his knees, staring at the floor.

“Elias?”

He didn’t respond.

Maya stepped closer.

“Elias.”

He finally looked up.

His eyes were filled with tears.

“You shouldn’t have remembered.”

Maya’s expression hardened.

“You’ve said that before.”

“I know.”

“I’m tired of secrets.”

“So am I.”

Elias stood.

Then he reached beneath his coat and pulled out a small ancient blade.

Maya stepped backward.

“What are you doing?”

He turned the blade around and offered her the handle.

“This belonged to your mother.”

Maya didn’t take it.

“Why give it to me now?”

“Because I’m not the Keeper anymore.”

A voice echoed through the darkness.

“You were never worthy of the title.”

The old man stepped forward.

Maya turned.

“You’re the Keeper?”

He nodded.

“The last one.”

“How long have you been protecting this place?”

The old man looked toward the mountain.

“Longer than your history remembers.”

Maya stared at him.

“You knew my mother.”

“I knew her better than anyone.”

“Then tell me the truth.”

The old man hesitated.

“She didn’t abandon you.”

Maya’s jaw tightened.

“Then why did she give me to the Shomrim?”

“Because they were the only ones who could hide you from what was hunting you.”

“What was hunting me?”

The old man looked at Elias.

“His father.”

Maya turned sharply.

Elias went pale.

“My father died before I was born.”

The old man shook his head.

“No.”

He stepped closer.

“Your father was one of the first Shomrim.”

Elias’s face changed.

“My father was a Keeper?”

“Worse.”

The old man lowered his voice.

“He believed the thing beneath the mountain should be awakened.”

Maya looked at Elias.

“And you?”

Elias didn’t answer.

The old man continued.

“Elias was sent to destroy you.”

Maya’s heart stopped.

“But he failed.”

“Why?”

Elias finally spoke.

“Because I couldn’t kill you.”

Silence.

Maya looked at him.

“You were supposed to kill me?”

“Yes.”

“Twenty-two years ago?”

“Yes.”

“And instead?”

“I hid you.”

The old man raised his staff.

“And that is why the Shomrim have hunted him ever since.”

A distant horn sounded above them.

Then another.

Then dozens.

Elias looked toward the entrance.

“They’re here.”

Maya gripped her mother’s blade.

“Who?”

The old man answered:

“The Shomrim.”

Maya stared into the darkness.

“How many?”

The old man smiled grimly.

“All of them.”

Then the mountain doors opened.

Hundreds of figures stood outside.

Their staffs burned with ancient symbols.

And at their center stood a woman Maya recognized immediately.

Her mother.

Alive.

Older.

And holding a sword.

She looked directly at Maya.

Then at Elias.

And whispered:

“Run.”

The Shomrim raised their weapons.

The mountain erupted.
