CHAPTER 11

THE CITY THAT REMEMBERED

The desert had gone silent.

Not peaceful.

Silent.

Maya stood at the edge of the excavation site, staring at the ruins buried beneath the sand. For eleven years she had studied fragments of civilizations that had disappeared without explanation.

But this was different.

This place had not disappeared.

It had been hidden.

Elias approached from behind, carrying the old leather case they had recovered from the chamber.

“You’re staring again.”

Maya didn’t turn.

“I’ve been looking at ruins my entire life.”

“And?”

“This one is looking back.”

Elias stopped.

For a moment neither of them spoke.

Then Maya noticed something in the sand.

A straight line.

She knelt and brushed away the dust.

Stone appeared beneath her fingers.

Then another line.

Then another.

Within minutes, a pattern emerged.

It was a symbol.

A circle divided by three lines.

Elias crouched beside her.

“That symbol…”

“You’ve seen it before?”

“In the manuscript.”

Maya looked at him.

“The one from the monastery?”

He nodded.

“The symbol wasn’t a name. It was a warning.”

Maya’s expression tightened.

“What did it say?”

Elias opened the leather case.

Inside was the fragment they had found beneath the ruins.

The surface had changed.

A faint light moved beneath it like something alive.

Elias carefully unfolded the manuscript.

“We translated this incorrectly.”

Maya frowned.

“How?”

“We thought the inscription said, Those who awaken the ancient ones will inherit their kingdom.”

“And?”

Elias pointed to the original text.

“It doesn’t say inherit.”

Maya leaned closer.

“What does it say?”

Elias looked toward the ruins.

“It says…”

He hesitated.

“Remember.”

A cold wind swept across the excavation.

The fragment suddenly became warm.

Maya pulled her hand away.

Then came a sound from beneath them.

Three knocks.

Knock.

Silence.

Knock.

Elias stood.

Knock.

Maya looked at him.

“That’s underneath us.”

Elias nodded.

“I know.”

The ground trembled.

Sand slid into a narrow opening that had appeared between two ancient stones.

Maya grabbed her flashlight.

“There’s a passage.”

Elias looked toward the horizon.

Dark clouds were forming in the distance.

“We don’t have much time.”

Maya stepped toward the opening.

“Then we go now.”

Elias caught her arm.

“Maya.”

She turned.

“What?”

“If this city was hidden for thousands of years…”

His eyes moved toward the darkness below.

“Someone may have hidden it for a reason.”

Maya looked down.

For the first time, she felt something she had not felt during eleven years of searching.

Fear.

Not fear of dying.

Fear of finding exactly what she had been looking for.

She lowered herself into the opening.

Elias followed.

The passage descended beneath the city.

The walls were covered with carvings.

Kings.

Wars.

Stars.

And a figure standing between them all.

A man.

Barefoot.

His hands were raised toward the heavens.

Maya stopped.

“Elias…”

He looked at the carving.

“What?”

“Look at his face.”

Elias lifted the flashlight.

The man’s face was damaged.

But beneath the broken stone was another inscription.

Maya brushed away the dust.

Four ancient letters appeared.

She whispered them.

“Yeshua.”

Elias stared at her.

“That can’t be.”

Maya looked deeper into the passage.

“Maybe we’ve been asking the wrong question.”

Behind them, the entrance suddenly collapsed.

Darkness swallowed the passage.

Elias turned.

“Maya…”

She raised the glowing fragment.

Its light illuminated a doorway ahead.

And carved above that doorway were five words:

BEFORE THE BEGINNING, HE WAITED.

Maya stepped forward.

The door opened by itself.

Knock knock
