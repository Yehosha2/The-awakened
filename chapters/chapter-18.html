CHAPTER 18

THE CITY RISES

The bells stopped.

Silence returned.

Maya stood beneath the ancient city, staring upward as dust drifted through the air.

Then the ground moved again.

Not a tremor.

A rise.

The entire city was lifting.

Elias grabbed one of the stone pillars.

“Maya! Hold on!”

The floor tilted violently.

Ancient buildings groaned as enormous chains of stone emerged from beneath them.

Maya watched in disbelief.

“The city was built on something.”

Elias looked down.

“No.”

A crack opened beneath his feet.

He stared into it.

His face went pale.

“It was built around something.”

A massive eye opened beneath the city.

Maya stopped breathing.

The eye was impossibly large.

It filled the darkness below them.

The same red light that had been burning inside the fragment now burned within the creature’s eye.

Elias whispered:

“That isn’t a creature.”

Maya looked at him.

“What is it?”

He shook his head.

“I don’t know.”

The voice came again.

This time, it came from inside Maya.

“You do.”

She closed her eyes.

Images flooded her mind.

A world before cities.

Before kingdoms.

Before written language.

A group of people standing beneath a sky filled with impossible stars.

And among them—

a woman holding a newborn child.

The woman looked exactly like Maya.

Maya screamed.

Elias caught her.

“What did you see?”

“My mother.”

Elias froze.

“Your mother?”

Maya opened her eyes.

“She wasn’t alone.”

The fragment floated between them.

A new image appeared within it.

Jonathan.

Young.

Standing beside Maya’s mother.

And behind them—

the same enormous eye.

Elias stared.

“Your father knew about this before you were born.”

Maya nodded slowly.

“And my mother…”

She swallowed.

“…was part of it.”

Suddenly, footsteps echoed through the ruins.

Elias raised his weapon.

Someone was approaching.

A figure emerged through the dust.

The stranger from the ancient city.

But now his face was visible.

Maya recognized him.

She had seen him in the photographs from her father’s journals.

Standing beside Jonathan.

Standing beside her mother.

Standing beside her.

The stranger smiled.

“Maya.”

Elias stepped between them.

“Who are you?”

The stranger ignored him.

His eyes remained fixed on Maya.

“You’ve finally awakened.”

Maya’s voice was cold.

“Where is my father?”

The stranger’s smile disappeared.

“He’s where he always intended to be.”

“Where?”

The stranger looked beneath them.

“Keeping the door closed.”

A deafening roar erupted below.

The ancient city shook.

The enormous eye opened wider.

The stranger whispered:

“But your father is losing.”

Maya looked down.

The red fragment began pulsing.

Once.

Twice.

Then three times.

The stranger stepped backward.

“Maya…”

“What?”

His face filled with fear.

“Whatever happens next…”

He looked directly into her eyes.

“Don’t let it remember you.”

Then the ground beneath Maya disappeared.
