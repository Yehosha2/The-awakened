CHAPTER XXX

THE CRADLE

Earth stood still

Maya couldn’t breathe.

Her mother stood beneath the ruined archway.

Alive.

Not a memory.

Not a vision.

Not a voice from the darkness.

Alive.

Maya took one step toward her.

“Mom?”

Her mother’s eyes filled with tears.

“Don’t come any closer.”

Maya stopped.

“Why?”

“Because they are watching you.”

Hundreds of Shomrim stood behind her.

Their faces were hidden beneath dark hoods.

Their staffs formed a wall of glowing symbols.

The old Keeper stepped forward.

“You broke the oath.”

Maya’s mother raised her sword.

“I broke it the day I realized what you intended to do.”

The Keeper’s expression hardened.

“You were warned.”

“I was a mother.”

Silence.

Maya stared at her.

“You gave me to them.”

Her mother looked at her.

“I gave you a chance to live.”

“You let them take me.”

“I had no choice.”

“You always had a choice.”

Her mother lowered the sword.

“I know.”

That answer hurt more than an excuse.

Maya stepped closer.

“Why did you erase my memories?”

Her mother looked toward the mountain.

“Because remembering would have brought it back.”

“What?”

“The thing that was inside you.”

Maya’s blood ran cold.

Elias stepped forward.

“Tell her everything.”

Her mother looked at him.

“You’re still alive.”

“Barely.”

She almost smiled.

“You were always difficult to kill.”

Then the mountain shook.

A deep sound rolled beneath them.

Everyone went silent.

The Shomrim turned toward the ground.

The old Keeper whispered:

“No.”

Maya felt the pendant against her chest begin to burn.

Her mother saw it.

“Take it off.”

Maya grabbed it.

“Why?”

“Because it’s calling to you.”

The pendant pulsed.

Once.

Twice.

Then—

BOOM.

A shockwave blasted through the ruins.

The Shomrim were thrown backward.

Maya fell.

When she looked up, the mountain had split open.

A staircase descended into absolute darkness.

Her mother stared at it.

“It has opened.”

Maya stood.

“What is down there?”

Her mother looked directly into her eyes.

“The place where you were born.”

Maya froze.

Elias whispered:

“No…”

Her mother nodded.

“The truth has waited long enough.”

She turned toward the staircase.

“Come with me.”

Maya hesitated.

“Why should I trust you?”

Her mother looked back.

“Because I am the only person alive who remembers what happened the first time.”

She stepped into the darkness.

Maya followed.

Elias came behind her.

The old Keeper remained at the entrance.

Maya glanced back.

“Are you coming?”

He shook his head.

“I have one last duty.”

“What duty?”

He raised his staff.

“To keep them out.”

The enormous stone doors began closing.

Hundreds of Shomrim rushed forward.

The old Keeper stood alone before them.

“Go!”

Maya turned and ran.

Behind her came the sound of battle.

Metal struck stone.

Ancient voices shouted.

Then—

silence.

Maya reached the bottom of the staircase.

Her mother was waiting beside a massive circular chamber.

At its center was a pool of perfectly still black water.

Maya approached.

“Is this where I was born?”

Her mother nodded.

“No.”

She pointed toward the water.

“This is where you were created.”

The surface of the water moved.

A reflection appeared.

Maya.

But not as she was now.

A younger version.

Then another.

And another.

Hundreds of versions of herself appeared beneath the surface.

Maya stepped backward.

“What am I looking at?”

Her mother whispered:

“Every life you have lived.”

The water suddenly went still.

One final reflection appeared.

A woman older than Maya.

Standing beside the first temple.

Holding a newborn child.

The woman turned toward Maya.

And smiled.

Maya’s mother grabbed her arm.

“Don’t look into her eyes.”

Too late.

The reflection opened its eyes.

And Maya heard her own voice come from beneath the water:

“This is not your first life.”

The pool exploded.
