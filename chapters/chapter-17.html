CHAPTER 17

THE AWAKENING

Maya stared at the inscription.

THE MONSTER WAS NEVER BENEATH THE CITY.

Her breathing became shallow.

IT WAS WITHIN.

Elias stepped away from her.

“Maya…”

She looked at him.

“What?”

“Your eyes.”

She touched her face.

“What about them?”

“They changed.”

Maya looked into the reflection of the ancient stone.

For a moment, she saw her own face.

Then the reflection smiled.

Maya didn’t.

She stumbled backward.

“No.”

The reflection raised its hand.

Maya’s hand remained at her side.

Elias saw it too.

“Don’t move.”

The reflection whispered:

“You remember me.”

Maya shook her head.

“I don’t know you.”

The reflection’s smile widened.

“You knew me before you were born.”

The chamber trembled.

Ancient symbols began appearing across Maya’s arms.

Elias grabbed her shoulders.

“Fight it.”

“I am!”

“No. You’re listening to it.”

Maya closed her eyes.

The voice became louder.

“Open the door.”

A memory suddenly struck her.

A little girl.

Five years old.

Standing beside her father.

Jonathan was kneeling in front of her, holding her shoulders.

Behind him was the same ancient symbol.

And he was crying.

Maya heard his younger voice:

“If you ever hear it calling your name, don’t answer.”

Her eyes opened.

“Dad knew.”

Elias stared at her.

“Knew what?”

Maya looked toward the sealed door.

“He knew this would happen.”

The fragment rose into the air.

A beam of red light struck the ceiling.

Stone began to split.

Above them, something enormous moved.

Elias whispered:

“We’re not underground anymore.”

Maya looked up.

“What?”

“The city is moving.”

The entire chamber tilted.

Buildings groaned.

Stone bridges collapsed.

The underground city was rising.

Maya grabbed Elias as the floor shifted beneath them.

Then a sound came from somewhere far above.

A bell.

One strike.

Then another.

Then hundreds.

Every bell in the ancient city began ringing.

And through the deafening sound, a voice spoke.

Not from the darkness.

From the sky.

“THE AWAKENED HAS RETURNED.”

Maya looked upward.

For the first time, she understood the truth.

They hadn’t discovered the ancient city.

The city had been waiting for her.

And now—

it was waking up.
