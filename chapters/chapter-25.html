CHAPTER TWENTY-FOUR

THE SECOND CITY

The staircase seemed to have no end.

Maya descended slowly.

Elias followed close behind.

Jonathan came next.

Adrian remained at the top.

Maya looked back.

“Aren't you coming?”

Adrian shook his head.

“I can't.”

“Why?”

He looked at the symbol on her hand.

“Because this place doesn't belong to me.”

Jonathan called from below.

“Maya!”

She turned and continued down.

The deeper they went, the colder the air became.

The walls changed.

Ancient stone became something darker.

Smoother.

Almost black.

Then Maya saw light.

She stopped.

At the bottom of the staircase was a massive underground city.

Buildings stretched across an enormous cavern.

Towers rose toward the ceiling.

Bridges connected structures that seemed impossible to build.

And everywhere Maya looked, the same symbol appeared.

On walls.

On doors.

On the streets.

Even in the center of the city.

“This is impossible,” Elias whispered.

Jonathan stared ahead.

“This is where it began.”

Maya stepped forward.

The moment her foot touched the ground, thousands of lights appeared throughout the city.

One after another.

Until the entire underground world was glowing.

Elias looked around.

“Did you do that?”

Maya shook her head.

“I don't know.”

A sound echoed through the city.

A bell.

Then another.

And another.

But these bells sounded different.

Lower.

Older.

Jonathan suddenly grabbed Maya.

“We have to leave.”

“Why?”

“Because the bells mean they're coming.”

Maya looked toward the distant buildings.

Shadows began moving between them.

Hundreds of them.

Elias reached for the fragment.

“People?”

Jonathan shook his head.

“Not anymore.”

The shadows stepped into the light.

They were tall.

Thin.

Their faces were covered by ancient masks.

Each mask carried the same symbol.

Maya felt the mark on her hand burning.

One of the masked figures raised its head.

Then all of them knelt.

Elias stared.

“Why are they kneeling?”

Jonathan's face went pale.

“Because they recognize her.”

Maya stepped forward.

“Who are you?”

The masked figures remained silent.

Then the largest one slowly stood.

It removed its mask.

Maya stopped breathing.

The face beneath it looked exactly like hers.

Elias whispered:

“Maya…”

The woman smiled.

“Finally.”

Maya couldn't speak.

The woman walked closer.

“You've been asleep for a very long time.”

Maya shook her head.

“Who are you?”

The woman looked toward Jonathan.

“He knows.”

Maya turned to her father.

Jonathan looked away.

“Dad?”

The woman smiled again.

“Ask him what happened the first time you died.”

Maya froze.

“The first time?”

Jonathan closed his eyes.

“Maya, there's something you need to know.”

The city suddenly went dark.

The woman's voice echoed through the cavern.

“She isn't his daughter.”

Maya looked at Jonathan.

Her heart stopped.

“Then whose daughter am I?”

Jonathan finally looked at her.

And whispered:

“The first one's.”
