CHAPTER XXXIV

MAYA, MAYA

Maya stared at the woman.

Same eyes.

Same face.

Even the small scar beneath her left eye.

Her mother grabbed Maya’s arm.

“Don’t go near her.”

The woman smiled.

“Why?”

Her voice was Maya’s.

Perfectly.

Maya felt the hairs rise on her arms.

“Who are you?”

The woman tilted her head.

“You.”

“No.”

“Yes.”

Elias stepped between them.

“Stay back.”

The woman looked at him.

“You always do that.”

Elias froze.

Maya noticed.

“You know her.”

“I know what she is.”

The woman laughed softly.

“You still haven’t told her.”

Maya looked at Elias.

“Told me what?”

The woman answered for him.

“He promised to kill me.”

Elias’s face went pale.

Maya’s mother whispered:

“Don’t listen.”

The woman took one step forward.

“I was the first Maya.”

Maya stared.

“What does that mean?”

“Before your mother gave you that name, I carried it.”

She touched her chest.

“I was the one who opened the first door.”

Maya felt a strange pull toward her.

A familiarity that frightened her more than anything else.

“You’re saying I’m a copy?”

The woman shook her head.

“No.”

She smiled.

“You are the continuation.”

The chamber trembled.

Maya’s mother raised her sword.

“You shouldn’t be here.”

The other Maya looked at her.

“You said that the last time.”

Her mother’s hands began shaking.

“What happened last time?”

Neither woman answered.

Maya looked between them.

“Tell me.”

Her mother lowered the sword.

“I couldn’t stop her.”

The other Maya stepped closer.

“You couldn’t stop me because you were the one who opened the door.”

Maya’s mother closed her eyes.

“I was trying to save my daughter.”

“You were trying to save yourself.”

The lights went out.

A scream echoed through the chamber.

Maya felt something brush past her.

Then silence.

“Mom?”

No answer.

“Elias?”

Nothing.

Maya reached into the darkness.

A hand caught hers.

She gasped.

The hand felt exactly like hers.

The other Maya whispered beside her:

“You want the truth?”

Maya couldn’t move.

“Yes.”

“Then stop asking your mother.”

A flash of light illuminated the chamber.

Her mother was gone.

Elias was gone.

Only the two Mayas remained.

The other Maya leaned closer.

“The Shomrim didn’t erase your memories.”

Maya’s heart pounded.

“Then who did?”

The woman smiled.

“You did.”

Maya shook her head.

“I was a child.”

“You weren’t always.”

The floor beneath them began glowing.

Ancient symbols formed a circle.

At its center appeared a single word.

REMEMBER.

The other Maya stepped into the circle.

“Come with me.”

“Where?”

“To the beginning.”

Maya hesitated.

“What happens if I do?”

The woman looked toward the darkness.

“You finally find out why you were born.”

She extended her hand.

Maya stared at it.

Then—

someone screamed her name from somewhere beneath the mountain.

Her mother’s voice.

“MAYA! RUN!”

The other Maya’s smile disappeared.

She whispered:

“It’s too late.”

The walls began closing.

And something enormous started climbing toward them.
