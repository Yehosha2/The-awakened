CHAPTER XXXI

THE QUIET BETWEEN

The water settled.

For the first time in what felt like hours, there was no screaming.

No shaking earth.

No ancient voice calling Maya’s name.

Only silence.

Maya stood beside the black pool, staring at her reflection.

Her mother remained a few steps away.

Neither spoke.

Finally, Maya whispered:

“Is it really me?”

Her mother nodded.

“Yes.”

“The woman in the water?”

“Yes.”

Maya looked down.

“How many lives?”

Her mother took a slow breath.

“I don’t know.”

“That isn’t an answer.”

“No.”

Her mother sat beside the pool.

“When you were born, I thought you were simply my daughter.”

Maya sat beside her.

“What changed?”

“I saw your eyes.”

Maya looked at her.

“My eyes?”

“You were only minutes old.”

Her mother’s voice softened.

“You looked at me as though you already knew me.”

Maya swallowed.

“I don’t remember.”

“I know.”

Her mother reached into her coat and removed a small piece of folded cloth.

She placed it in Maya’s hand.

Maya unfolded it.

Inside was a tiny silver pendant.

The same symbol she had seen throughout the mountain.

“You kept it.”

“I couldn’t throw it away.”

“Why?”

“Because it was the last thing that reminded me that you were my daughter.”

Maya looked at her mother.

“And what were you afraid I would become?”

Her mother looked toward the dark water.

“Something powerful.”

Maya waited.

“But power wasn’t what frightened me.”

“What was?”

Her mother’s eyes filled with tears.

“Loneliness.”

Maya looked down.

For the first time, she understood.

Her mother hadn’t only been afraid of what Maya might become.

She had been afraid of Maya becoming something that no one could understand.

Maya reached over and took her mother’s hand.

Her mother hesitated.

Then held it.

They sat together beside the water.

No ancient voices.

No Keepers.

No secrets spoken in the dark.

Just a mother and daughter who had lost twenty-two years.

“I missed you,” Maya whispered.

Her mother closed her eyes.

“I missed every day.”

Maya leaned against her shoulder.

For a moment, the mountain seemed almost peaceful.

Then the black water rippled.

Neither woman moved.

A single circle spread across the surface.

Then another.

Maya slowly lifted her head.

Her mother whispered:

“Don’t.”

But Maya was already looking.

A small hand appeared beneath the surface.

Not reaching upward.

Waving.

Maya stared.

Her mother squeezed her hand.

“Whatever you hear…”

The hand disappeared.

“…don’t answer.”

From beneath the water came a child’s voice.

Soft.

Almost gentle.

“Mother?”

Maya’s eyes filled with tears.

Her mother whispered:

“That’s not your child.”

Maya looked at her.

“Then whose is it?”

The water became perfectly still.

And a second voice answered from the darkness:

“Yours.”
