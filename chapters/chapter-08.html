CHAPTER EIGHT

MAYA

The fourth knock never came.

The silence that followed was worse.

Maya stood frozen, still holding the photograph.

Her own face stared back at her.

The photograph had been taken that morning.

But the handwriting on the back was what frightened her.

YOU ARE NOT THE FIRST MAYA.

She turned toward Elias.

“What does it mean?”

He didn’t answer.

“Elias.”

The professor finally looked at her.

“It means your name has appeared before.”

Maya shook her head.

“My name?”

“Yes.”

“In what?”

Elias opened his father’s notebook.

He turned several pages.

Then stopped.

There, beneath a series of ancient symbols, was a name written in faded ink.

Maya.

Maya stared at it.

“That isn’t possible.”

“I know.”

“When was this written?”

Elias looked at the date.

“1951.”

David stepped closer.

“That could be coincidence.”

Elias shook his head.

“Look beneath it.”

Maya leaned forward.

Another name had been written below hers.

Maya Reed.

Her stomach tightened.

“My last name?”

Elias nodded.

She took the notebook from him.

The page was old.

The handwriting was unmistakably different from her own.

Yet the name was hers.

Exactly.

“How?”

Elias looked away.

“My father spent years trying to answer that question.”

Maya turned another page.

There were photographs.

Black-and-white images of archaeological sites.

Old excavation teams.

Ancient ruins.

Then she saw a photograph of a woman.

She was standing beside a trench.

Maya’s breath stopped.

The woman looked like her.

Not similar.

Not vaguely familiar.

The same eyes.

The same shape of her face.

Even the small scar above her eyebrow.

Maya touched the photograph.

“Who is she?”

Elias answered quietly.

“We don’t know.”

Maya looked at him.

“You don’t know?”

“My father called her the First Maya.”

David stared at the photograph.

“How old is this?”

“Taken in 1951.”

Maya shook her head.

“That woman can’t be me.”

“No.”

Elias pointed to the date written beneath the photograph.

“She was born in 1928.”

Maya stepped backward.

The room seemed to tilt.

Then she noticed something else.

Around the woman’s neck was a small pendant.

A circular symbol.

Maya reached beneath her shirt.

Her fingers found the chain around her own neck.

She pulled it into the light.

The same symbol hung there.

Elias stared at it.

“Where did you get that?”

“My mother gave it to me.”

“Where did she get it?”

“I don’t know.”

Maya looked at the photograph again.

The woman in the picture was wearing the same pendant.

Elias slowly sat down.

“My father was right.”

Maya turned toward him.

“About what?”

He looked at her.

“Some bloodlines aren’t inherited.”

Maya frowned.

“What does that mean?”

Elias looked toward the ancient tablet.

“They’re remembered.”

A sudden sound came from outside.

David looked toward the entrance.

“What now?”

No one answered.

Then a man’s voice spoke from beyond the tent.

“Professor Ben-Ami.”

Elias froze.

Maya recognized the fear in his expression.

“Who is that?”

Elias whispered:

“My father’s voice.”

The tent went silent.

Then the voice spoke again.

“Maya.”

She slowly turned toward the entrance.

“Don’t go out there,” Elias said.

But the voice continued.

“You’ve been looking for us.”

Maya stared at the darkness.

“I’ve never seen you before.”

A pause.

Then:

“Yes, you have.”

The lights went out.

And in the darkness, Maya heard someone whisper directly into her ear:

“Just not in this life.”
