CHAPTER NINETEEN

THE FALL

Maya fell.

There was no time to scream.

The ancient city vanished above her as darkness swallowed everything.

Then—

Elias grabbed her hand.

His fingers locked around her wrist.

“Maya!”

She looked up.

He was hanging from the broken edge of the city, one hand gripping a stone ledge, the other holding her.

“Don’t let go!”

“I can’t!”

The stone beneath Elias’s hand began cracking.

Far below them, the enormous eye watched.

It wasn’t blinking.

It was waiting.

Maya looked down.

“Elias…”

“What?”

“It knows we’re here.”

“I know!”

“No.”

She stared into the eye.

“It knows me.”

The creature beneath them moved.

The entire cavern shook.

Elias pulled with everything he had.

Maya felt herself rising.

Then something touched her ankle.

She looked down.

A black hand had emerged from the darkness.

It wrapped around her leg.

Maya screamed.

Elias pulled harder.

“LET HER GO!”

The hand tightened.

Maya felt something entering her mind.

A voice whispered:

“Remember.”

Suddenly she saw everything.

Her father standing in the ancient chamber.

Her mother beside him.

A newborn Maya lying between them.

And something else standing behind them.

A shadow.

A presence.

Something they were terrified of.

Jonathan looked at the newborn child.

Then at the shadow.

And said:

“She is the only one who can open the door.”

Maya’s eyes widened.

“No…”

The memory changed.

Jonathan was older.

He was running through the desert.

Behind him, people were chasing him.

He was carrying the same fragment.

Then he stopped.

Turned toward the camera.

And spoke directly to Maya.

“If you’re seeing this, I’m already gone.”

The vision shattered.

Maya screamed.

The black hand released her.

Elias pulled her over the ledge.

They collapsed onto the stone floor.

For several seconds, neither spoke.

Then Maya whispered:

“My father left me a message.”

Elias looked at her.

“What did he say?”

Maya stared into the darkness.

“He said I’m the one who can open the door.”

Elias slowly stood.

“Then we’re not going to open it.”

A voice answered from behind them.

“You already did.”

They turned.

The stranger stood in the ruins.

But he wasn’t alone.

Behind him stood dozens of people.

All wearing the same symbol.

The symbol on Maya’s hand.

The stranger looked at her.

“They’ve been waiting for you.”

Maya rose slowly.

“Who are they?”

The stranger smiled.

“The ones who remember.”

Then every person behind him knelt.

And together they whispered:

“Welcome back.”
