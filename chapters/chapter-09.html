CHAPTER 9

THE MAN IN THE DARK

Maya couldn’t move.

The whisper was still beside her ear.

Just not in this life.

Then the lights came back.

The tent was empty.

David stood near the entrance, breathing heavily.

Elias was staring at the doorway.

Maya turned slowly.

“Where did he go?”

Elias didn’t answer.

“Elias.”

“I don’t know.”

“You said that was your father’s voice.”

“It was.”

“But your father is dead.”

“Yes.”

“Then explain it.”

Elias looked at her.

“I can’t.”

Outside, footsteps moved across the sand.

Slow.

Deliberate.

Getting farther away.

David grabbed his flashlight.

“I’m going after him.”

“No,” Elias said.

David stopped.

“Why?”

“Because that’s what he wants.”

Maya looked at Elias.

“Who?”

Elias closed his father’s notebook.

“The man who has been following us.”

“You know who he is?”

“I know what my father called him.”

“And?”

Elias looked toward the darkness.

“The Witness.”

Maya frowned.

“The Witness?”

“He appeared every time my father found something connected to the tablet.”

David shook his head.

“That doesn’t tell us anything.”

“It tells us enough.”

Elias pointed toward the photograph.

“My father believed the Witness wasn’t protecting the secret.”

Maya looked at him.

“Then what was he doing?”

Elias’s expression hardened.

“Waiting for someone to discover it.”

A sound came from outside.

Metal striking stone.

Maya stepped toward the entrance.

Elias caught her arm.

“Stay here.”

Then they heard it.

A vehicle.

An engine starting somewhere beyond the ruins.

David ran outside.

A few seconds later he shouted.

“They left something!”

Maya and Elias followed.

David stood beside a black case lying in the dust.

There were no footprints around it.

No explanation for how it had appeared.

Elias stared at the case.

“Don’t touch it.”

David looked at him.

“Why?”

“Because nothing connected to this mystery has ever been left for us by accident.”

Maya crouched beside the case.

There was a symbol carved into the lid.

The same symbol.

The seal.

The tablet.

The pendant.

All connected.

Maya reached toward it.

Elias stopped her.

“Wait.”

He examined the hinges.

Then the lock.

There was no keyhole.

Instead, there was a small circular indentation.

Maya slowly removed her pendant.

Elias stared.

“No.”

“What?”

“The pendant.”

She held it above the indentation.

“Don’t.”

But Maya already understood.

She lowered it.

The pendant fit perfectly.

The case clicked.

The lock opened.

Inside was a single object.

A photograph.

Maya picked it up.

Her hands began to shake.

It showed the same woman from Elias’s father’s notebook.

The First Maya.

But this photograph was different.

She wasn’t standing beside an excavation.

She was standing in front of the ancient doorway.

And beside her stood a man.

Maya looked at Elias.

“Who is he?”

Elias took the photograph.

His face changed.

“I know him.”

“Who?”

“My father.”

Maya stared at the photograph.

“But you said your father disappeared from the site in 1951.”

“He did.”

David looked between them.

“Then how is he standing in this photograph?”

Elias turned the photograph over.

There was a date written on the back.

2026.

Nobody spoke.

Maya felt the blood drain from her face.

“That photograph was taken this year.”

Elias looked toward the distant ruins.

Then he whispered:

“My father wasn’t the one who disappeared.”

Maya waited.

Elias looked at her.

“He was the one who came back.”
