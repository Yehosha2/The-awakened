CHAPTER TEN

THE MAN WHO RETURNED

The doorway continued to glow.

Maya couldn’t look away.

The photograph trembled in her hand.

Her eyes remained fixed on the man standing beside the First Maya.

Elias’s father.

But the date on the back of the photograph was impossible.

2026.

David stepped closer.

“Professor…”

Elias didn’t respond.

“Elias.”

The professor finally looked at him.

“I know.”

“How?”

“I don’t.”

Maya turned the photograph over again.

“Maybe the date is wrong.”

Elias shook his head.

“My father never made mistakes with dates.”

“Then someone changed it.”

“No.”

“Why are you so certain?”

Elias looked at the photograph.

“Because that’s his handwriting.”

Silence.

The blue light from the doorway spread across the ruins.

Maya noticed something moving inside the entrance.

A shadow.

Then another.

David raised his flashlight.

“Someone’s coming.”

Elias grabbed his arm.

“Don’t.”

A figure appeared.

An elderly man stepped out of the darkness.

His clothing was old.

Dust covered his shoulders.

His face was pale.

But his eyes were clear.

Elias stopped breathing.

The man looked directly at him.

“Elias.”

The professor’s knees almost gave way.

“Father?”

Maya stared.

The man smiled.

“You’ve grown.”

Elias couldn’t speak.

“My father died forty-eight years ago.”

The old man looked toward the tablet.

“Not here.”

“What happened to you?”

The man’s expression became serious.

“I went through.”

Maya stepped forward.

“Through what?”

The old man looked at her.

For a moment, he seemed frightened.

Then he whispered:

“You.”

Maya froze.

“Me?”

“You were always the key.”

Elias stepped between them.

“What did you do to her?”

His father looked at him.

“I didn’t do anything.”

“Then why does she have the mark?”

The old man looked at Maya’s pendant.

“Because she was chosen before she was born.”

Maya’s heart raced.

“Chosen by who?”

The old man looked toward the doorway.

“They are awake.”

A deep vibration moved through the ground.

Dust fell from the ancient stones.

David stepped backward.

“What is happening?”

Elias’s father looked at the darkness.

“The door is opening.”

Maya stared at the doorway.

“Where does it lead?”

The old man answered:

“Not where.”

He looked directly into her eyes.

“When.”

The ground shook again.

The tablet began to glow.

Symbols appeared across its surface.

Then the ancient clay spoke.

Not with sound.

With light.

A single image appeared above it.

A city.

Not Jericho.

Not any city Maya recognized.

A vast ancient city beneath a sky filled with unfamiliar stars.

Maya whispered:

“What is that?”

Elias stared at the vision.

“My father told me this place never existed.”

His father corrected him.

“It existed before your history began.”

The image changed.

A figure appeared within the city.

A man standing beneath an enormous tree.

Maya felt something inside her recognize him.

She whispered the name before she understood why.

“Yehoshua.”

The old man closed his eyes.

“Yes.”

The vision disappeared.

The doorway went dark.

Then a voice came from beneath the earth.

Not the voice of Elias’s father.

Not the Witness.

Something older.

Something that seemed to speak from everywhere at once.

“The memory has returned.”

Maya looked at Elias.

The professor was staring at her.

“What?”

He pointed toward her hand.

Maya looked down.

The pendant was glowing.

And beneath the ancient symbol, a second mark had appeared.

A mark shaped like an open eye.

Then, from somewhere beyond the doorway—

something whispered her name.
