CHAPTER XXXIII

THE MOTHER’S SECRET

Maya stood over the black water.

The voice had stopped.

But its final words remained inside her.

Then keep your promise.

Her mother pulled her away from the pool.

“We have to go.”

Maya didn’t move.

“You knew about the promise.”

Her mother said nothing.

“You knew.”

“Yes.”

“Why didn’t you tell me?”

“Because I was afraid you would remember.”

Maya turned toward her.

“I already did.”

Her mother’s face crumpled.

“I know.”

A distant rumble moved through the chamber.

The black water began draining into the stone beneath them.

Maya watched it disappear.

“What was behind the door?”

Her mother looked at the empty pool.

“A place without time.”

Maya frowned.

“What does that mean?”

“It means you can enter yesterday and return tomorrow.”

“That isn’t possible.”

“Neither is what you are.”

Maya fell silent.

Her mother took the silver pendant from Maya’s hand.

“This belonged to your grandmother.”

Maya stared.

“I never knew her.”

“You weren’t supposed to.”

“Why?”

“Because she was the first person to find the door.”

Maya’s eyes widened.

“She found it?”

“Yes.”

“And what happened to her?”

Her mother looked away.

“She went through.”

“Did she come back?”

A long silence.

“No.”

Maya swallowed.

“Then how did you know what was on the other side?”

Her mother finally looked at her.

“Because she sent something back.”

“What?”

Her mother opened the pendant.

Inside was a tiny piece of black stone.

“The stone.”

Maya stared at it.

“The same stone from the cradle?”

Her mother nodded.

“It arrived three days after she disappeared.”

Maya touched the stone.

It was warm.

Her mother quickly pulled her hand away.

“Don’t.”

“Why?”

“Because that’s how it begins.”

“What begins?”

“Remembering.”

A sound echoed behind them.

Footsteps.

Slow.

Measured.

Maya turned.

Elias stood at the entrance.

His clothes were torn.

Blood covered his face.

But he was alive.

“Maya.”

She ran toward him.

Her mother stepped between them.

“No.”

Elias stopped.

“She’s my daughter.”

Maya froze.

Her mother’s face changed.

Elias looked at her.

“You finally told her.”

Maya turned slowly.

“Your daughter?”

Her mother looked at Elias.

Then back at Maya.

“No.”

Elias whispered:

“Not mine.”

Maya’s confusion deepened.

“Then whose?”

Elias looked toward the pendant.

His voice dropped.

“That’s the question we’ve been trying to answer for twenty-two years.”

A deep sound came from beneath the chamber.

The three of them looked down.

The black stone inside the pendant began glowing.

Maya heard a heartbeat.

Then another.

Then hundreds.

Her mother’s eyes widened.

“No.”

Elias backed away.

“What?”

She whispered:

“The door is opening.”

The chamber wall split apart.

Beyond it was a corridor that hadn’t existed before.

At the far end stood a figure.

A woman.

Waiting.

Maya stared.

She looked exactly like her.

Same face.

Same eyes.

Same scar beneath her left eye.

Elias whispered:

“That’s impossible.”

The woman smiled.

And Maya heard her own voice say:

“Come home.”
