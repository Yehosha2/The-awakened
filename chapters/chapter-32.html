CHAPTER XXXII

THE VOICE BENEATH THE WATER

Maya didn’t answer.

She stared into the black pool.

Her mother’s hand remained wrapped around hers.

“Don’t listen,” her mother whispered.

The water rippled again.

“Mother…”

Maya closed her eyes.

The voice sounded so small.

So frightened.

Almost human.

Her mother pulled her away from the pool.

“You can’t save everything that calls for you.”

Maya looked at her.

“How do you know?”

“Because I tried.”

Silence.

Maya studied her mother’s face.

“You heard it before.”

Her mother nodded.

“Many times.”

“What did it tell you?”

“That it was alone.”

Maya looked back at the water.

“And you believed it?”

“For a while.”

A tear slipped down her mother’s cheek.

“That’s how it gets close.”

The water became still.

Then a reflection appeared.

Not Maya.

Not her mother.

Elias.

He stood beneath the surface as though trapped behind glass.

Maya rushed toward the pool.

“Elias!”

Her mother grabbed her.

“Wait.”

Elias raised his hand.

His mouth moved.

No sound came.

Maya leaned closer.

“What is he saying?”

Her mother stared at the reflection.

“He’s warning us.”

“About what?”

The reflection suddenly changed.

Elias disappeared.

The old Keeper appeared.

Then the Shomrim.

Then a vast stone chamber.

And finally—

a door.

A door Maya recognized.

The same door from her childhood memory.

Her mother’s face went pale.

“No.”

“What?”

“That door was destroyed.”

Maya stared at the reflection.

“Then why is it here?”

The surface of the water darkened.

Words appeared.

THE FIRST DOOR WAS NEVER DESTROYED.

Maya whispered:

“Where is it?”

The answer appeared beneath it.

WITHIN YOU.

Her mother stepped backward.

“No…”

Maya turned.

“Mom?”

Her mother looked terrified.

“I thought they removed it.”

“Removed what?”

“The doorway.”

Maya’s pulse quickened.

“You said there was something inside me.”

Her mother nodded.

“A place.”

“A place?”

“A doorway to somewhere that should not exist.”

The pool began to tremble.

Maya looked down.

The reflection of herself appeared again.

But this Maya was older.

She was standing before the ancient door.

And beside her stood Elias.

They were holding hands.

Maya stared.

“We’ve done this before.”

Her mother whispered:

“Yes.”

“When?”

Her mother closed her eyes.

“Before you were born.”

The black water rose from the pool.

It formed a human shape.

A face emerged.

The face of the child.

“Mother…”

Maya stepped forward.

Her mother shouted:

“DON’T!”

The child smiled.

Then it whispered:

“You promised you would come back.”

Maya froze.

A memory surfaced.

Not from her mother.

Not from Elias.

From herself.

A little girl standing before the ancient door.

A little girl placing her hand against it.

And saying:

“I promise.”

The memory shattered.

Maya staggered.

Her mother caught her.

“What did you see?”

Maya looked at her.

Her voice was barely audible.

“I remembered making a promise.”

Her mother held her tighter.

“To whom?”

Maya looked at the water.

The child was gone.

But something else remained beneath the surface.

A pair of enormous eyes.

Maya whispered:

“To whatever is on the other side.”

The eyes opened wider.

And the voice answered:

“Then keep your promise.”
