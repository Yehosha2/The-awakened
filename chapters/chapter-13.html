CHAPTER 13

ONE WHO REMEMBERED

The darkness lasted only three seconds.

But Maya felt as though she had fallen through centuries.

Then the lights returned.

One by one.

The stranger was gone.

Elias kept his weapon raised.

“Where did he go?”

Maya looked toward the empty entrance.

“He was standing right there.”

“I know.”

A low vibration passed through the floor.

Maya looked down.

The glowing fragment in her hand had changed again.

A thin line of light was moving across its surface.

It formed a symbol.

The same three lines inside a circle they had discovered above ground.

But now there was something beneath it.

A name.

ELIAS.

Maya stared at him.

“Why does it know your name?”

Elias didn’t answer.

“Elias?”

He slowly lowered his weapon.

“I’ve seen that symbol before.”

“Where?”

“In my father’s journals.”

Maya’s expression changed.

“You never told me your father studied this.”

“He didn’t.”

“Then why was it in his journals?”

Elias looked toward the ancient city.

“Because he wasn’t studying it.”

A distant rumble interrupted him.

Maya stepped closer.

“What was he doing?”

Elias finally looked at her.

“Trying to destroy it.”

The floor suddenly split.

A crack raced between them.

Maya jumped backward as stone collapsed into a bottomless shaft.

Elias grabbed her hand.

For several seconds, neither moved.

Then something rose from the darkness below.

Not a person.

A staircase.

Ancient stone steps emerged from the earth, slowly revealing a path downward.

Maya stared.

“There is another level.”

Elias shook his head.

“No.”

“What?”

“That isn’t a staircase.”

He pointed toward the walls.

The carvings were moving.

Figures carved into the stone were turning their heads.

All of them were looking downward.

Maya whispered:

“They’re afraid.”

Elias nodded.

“Whatever is down there…”

He paused.

“…they were trying to keep it there.”

A voice suddenly echoed through the city.

This time, it was unmistakably human.

“Maya.”

She froze.

The voice came from below.

“Maya Reed.”

Elias grabbed her shoulder.

“Don’t answer.”

The voice came again.

“You’ve searched for me for eleven years.”

Maya’s face went pale.

She knew that voice.

She had heard it only once.

Eleven years ago.

The night her father disappeared.

Maya stepped toward the staircase.

“Dad?”

Elias caught her.

“Maya, wait.”

But she was already crying.

The voice spoke one final time.

“Come down.”

The glowing fragment burned brighter than ever.

And somewhere beneath the ancient city—

a door began to open.
