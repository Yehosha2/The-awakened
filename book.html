Chapter One

The Tablet

Maya Reed had spent eleven years searching for things that no longer existed.

Cities buried beneath sand.

Names worn from stone.

Languages no one spoke anymore.

She had learned that archaeology rarely rewarded patience. Most days consisted of dust, broken pottery, fragments of walls, and objects that told you almost nothing.

But sometimes—

very rarely—

the earth gave something back.

That morning, Maya was kneeling at the edge of an excavation trench when her brush touched something hard.

She stopped.

“Don’t move,” David said behind her.

Maya smiled.

“I wasn’t planning to.”

David crouched beside her.

“Another piece of pottery?”

“No.”

She brushed away another layer of soil.

A curved surface appeared.

Brown.

Dry.

Ancient.

David leaned closer.

“Clay?”

Maya nodded.

“Looks like it.”

She continued working.

Slowly, the object emerged.

It was smaller than her palm.

A tablet.

But something about it was wrong.

Maya stared at the surface.

The markings were not ordinary cuneiform.

At least, not entirely.

She recognized the wedge impressions.

But between them were other signs.

Angular.

Deliberate.

Almost familiar.

David noticed her expression.

“What?”

Maya didn’t answer.

She carefully lifted the tablet from the earth.

For several seconds she simply held it.

Then she turned it over.

There was writing on the back.

✦ ✦ ✦

David’s voice became quieter.

“Maya?”

She looked at him.

“This shouldn’t be here.”

“Why?”

Maya looked down at the tablet.

“In three different places.”

David stared at her.

“What does that mean?”

“It means I’ve seen these symbols before.”

“Where?”

Maya hesitated.

“On photographs of fragments from three separate collections.”

David frowned.

“And you’re sure?”

“No.”

She looked again at the tablet.

“That’s the problem.”

That evening, Maya placed the tablet beneath the examination light.

She photographed every mark.

Then she opened an old research file she had carried with her for years.

Three photographs appeared on her screen.

Different excavations.

Different locations.

Different centuries according to the archaeological records.

Yet the same strange symbols appeared in each.

Maya stared at the screen.

There was only one person she could think of who might understand what she was looking at.

Professor Elias Ben-Ami.

A historian of ancient languages and civilizations, Elias had spent decades studying texts that most scholars considered marginal, corrupted, or impossible to interpret.

Maya had met him once.

Years earlier.

He had told her something she had never forgotten.

“When an ancient text doesn’t make sense, don’t assume the ancient people were wrong.”

She opened her contacts.

Her finger hovered over his name.

Then she called.

The phone rang once.

Twice.

Three times.

A man’s voice answered.

“Professor Ben-Ami.”

“Professor, it’s Maya Reed.”

Silence.

Then—

“Maya.”

His voice changed.

“You don’t call unless you’ve found something.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Maya looked at the tablet beneath the light.

“I think I have.”

Another silence.

“What did you find?”

Maya glanced at the symbols.

“I don’t know.”

Elias breathed slowly.

“Then send me a photograph.”

“I already did.”

He paused.

“I’ll be there tomorrow.”

Maya looked toward the darkened excavation beyond the window.

“Professor…”

“Yes?”

“There is something else.”

“What?”

Maya lowered her voice.

“The symbols aren’t the oldest thing on the tablet.”

Elias said nothing.

Maya looked at the back of the clay.

“There is another inscription beneath them.”

“What does it say?”

Maya stared at the ancient marks.

“I haven’t translated it.”

“Then how do you know it’s an inscription?”

“Because…”

She swallowed.

“I recognize the name.”

Elias’s voice became almost a whisper.

“What name?”

“Yehoshua”

Chapter Two

The Professor

Professor Elias Ben-Ami arrived before sunrise.

Maya had expected an older man carrying a briefcase filled with books.

Instead, a dark vehicle stopped outside the excavation compound and Elias stepped out carrying only a worn leather satchel.

He looked toward the excavation.

Then toward Maya.

For several seconds neither of them spoke.

“You said the name was Yehoshua,” he finally said.

Maya nodded.

“I did.”

Elias walked toward her.

“Show me.”

Maya led him into the examination tent.

The tablet rested beneath a glass cover.

Elias stopped when he saw it.

The expression on his face changed.

Not surprise.

Recognition.

Maya noticed immediately.

“You’ve seen it before.”

Elias didn’t answer.

He moved closer.

✦ ✦ ✦

His eyes followed the markings across the clay.

“Where did you get this?”

“Yesterday.”

“I mean where?”

Maya gave him the excavation coordinates.

Elias stared at her.

“That isn’t possible.”

“That’s what I said.”

He removed his glasses.

“Who else has touched it?”

“David and me.”

“Anyone else?”

“No.”

“Good.”

Maya frowned.

“Why?”

“Because until we understand what this is, nobody else should.”

David entered the tent.

“You guys want coffee?”

Neither answered.

David looked between them.

“What?”

Elias pointed toward the tablet.

“Have you ever seen anything like this?”

David shook his head.

“I’m an archaeologist, Professor. I’ve seen a lot of clay.”

Elias smiled faintly.

“This isn’t about the clay.”

He leaned closer.

“It’s about the language.”

Maya folded her arms.

“You recognize it?”

“Parts of it.”

“Cuneiform?”

“Some of it.”

“And the rest?”

Elias looked at Maya.

“That is what concerns me.”

He carefully examined the tablet without touching it.

“These symbols appear to have been written by someone familiar with several ancient writing systems.”

Maya stepped closer.

“Mesopotamian?”

“Partly.”

“Hebrew?”

“Not exactly.”

“Samaritan?”

Elias paused.

“Closer.”

Maya’s eyes narrowed.

“Then what is it?”

✦ ✦ ✦

“I don’t know.”

That answer unsettled her more than anything else.

Elias was famous for knowing.

He had spent forty years studying languages that had disappeared thousands of years ago.

Yet he was staring at a tablet as though it had violated everything he understood about history.

Maya pointed toward the rear of the tablet.

“You said you recognized the name.”

Elias nodded.

“Yehoshua.”

“What does it mean?”

Elias looked at her.

“It is an ancient form of a name you already know.”

“Jesus?”

Elias didn’t immediately answer.

“Yes.”

The tent became silent.

David looked confused.

“Jesus?”

Elias nodded.

“But this tablet predates the first century by centuries.”

Maya stared at him.

“Then how can his name be here?”

Elias looked down at the tablet.

“That is the question.”

✦ ✦ ✦

He finally opened his leather satchel.

Inside were photographs, handwritten notes, and several old reproductions of ancient inscriptions.

He placed three photographs on the table.

Maya recognized them immediately.

The first was from a museum collection in Baghdad.

The second came from an excavation in Israel.

The third was from a private archive in Europe.

Different places.

Different discoveries.

The same symbols.

Maya looked at Elias.

“You knew about these.”

“I’ve been studying them for twenty-seven years.”

“Why didn’t anyone publish this?”

Elias looked toward the entrance of the tent.

“Because every scholar who gets close to this question eventually discovers that the evidence doesn’t fit the accepted timeline.”

Maya looked back at the tablet.

“What timeline?”

Elias lowered his voice.

“The beginning.”

A cold feeling moved through Maya.

“The beginning of what?”

Elias looked directly at her.

“Everything we think we know about humanity.”

✦ ✦ ✦

He placed one finger beside the inscription.

“This isn’t a record of a king.”

“Then what is it?”

Elias studied the ancient marks.

“A memory.”

Maya stared at him.

“A memory of what?”

Elias didn’t answer.

Outside, the first light of morning appeared over the desert.

And beneath that light, something buried for thousands of years had begun to speak again.

✦ ✦ ✦
Chapter Three

The Hidden Language

The sun had risen completely by the time Elias finished examining the tablet.

Maya hadn’t moved.

Neither had David.

The three of them stood around the examination table, watching the ancient clay as though it might suddenly reveal its secret.

Elias finally spoke.

“We need to leave.”

Maya looked at him.

“Leave?”

“Not the excavation.”

He pointed toward the tablet.

“This.”

Maya frowned.

“You think it’s dangerous?”

“I think we don’t understand it.”

“That’s not an answer.”

“It is the only honest one I have.”

Elias carefully placed the photographs back inside his satchel.

Maya crossed her arms.

“You’ve spent twenty-seven years studying these symbols. You must know something.”

“I know enough to be concerned.”

He looked at the tablet again.

✦ ✦ ✦

“There are three layers of writing.”

Maya leaned closer.

“Three?”

“Yes.”

“The cuneiform is the first.”

“Correct.”

“And the angular symbols?”

“The second.”

“What about the third?”

Elias hesitated.

“You haven’t seen it yet.”

Maya stared at him.

“What do you mean?”

Elias turned the tablet toward her.

“Look at the spaces between the symbols.”

Maya bent over the table.

At first she saw nothing.

Then she noticed it.

Tiny marks.

Almost invisible.

They were not impressed into the clay like the other writing.

They appeared to have been scratched into it afterward.

Maya’s breathing slowed.

“That’s another language.”

Elias nodded.

✦ ✦ ✦

“Or something pretending to be one.”

David stepped closer.

“Can you translate it?”

Elias shook his head.

“Not yet.”

Maya looked at him.

“But you recognize the pattern.”

“Yes.”

“From where?”

Elias opened his satchel again.

This time he removed a small notebook.

The cover was cracked with age.

He opened it carefully.

Inside were pages filled with handwritten symbols.

Maya stared.

The symbols were almost identical to those on the tablet.

She looked at Elias.

“You’ve been keeping this?”

“For decades.”

“Where did you find them?”

Elias closed the notebook.

“I didn’t.”

Maya waited.

“Then who did?”

Elias looked toward the desert.

✦ ✦ ✦

“My father.”

Silence filled the tent.

“He was an archaeologist?”

“Yes.”

“Where did he find them?”

Elias didn’t answer immediately.

Then he said:

“Jericho.”

Maya’s eyes widened.

“When?”

“1947.”

David looked at him.

“That was before the Dead Sea Scrolls were discovered.”

“I know.”

Maya stared at the notebook.

“What happened to the discovery?”

“My father was told to forget about it.”

“By whom?”

Elias looked at her.

“That is what I’ve spent my life trying to find out.”

✦ ✦ ✦

A sudden crack came from the examination table.

Everyone froze.

Maya looked down.

A thin line had appeared across the surface of the tablet.

David stepped backward.

“What was that?”

Elias moved closer.

The crack continued.

Slowly.

Silently.

Then the clay tablet split.

Maya gasped.

The inside was hollow.

Something had been hidden inside it.

A small piece of black stone fell onto the table.

Elias stared at it.

For the first time since arriving, the professor looked genuinely afraid.

Maya picked up the stone.

“What is this?”

Elias grabbed her wrist.

“Don’t.”

She stopped.

His eyes remained fixed on the object.

“That isn’t a stone.”

“Then what is it?”

Elias whispered:

“It is a seal.”

Maya turned it over.

A single symbol had been carved into its surface.

A symbol neither of them recognized.

✦ ✦ ✦
Chapter Four

The Father’s Notebook

Elias did not speak for several seconds.

He kept staring at the seal in Maya’s hand.

“What did your father’s notebook say?” Maya asked.

Elias looked toward her.

“I don’t know.”

“You just said you’ve seen this symbol before.”

“I said I’ve seen the symbol.”

He carefully took the seal from her.

“My father’s notebook disappeared before I could read the final pages.”

David frowned.

“Disappeared?”

Elias nodded.

“He died in 1978.”

Maya watched him carefully.

“How?”

“Heart failure.”

“And the notebook?”

“Gone.”

“Someone took it?”

“I never proved that.”

Elias placed the seal beside the broken tablet.

“But I knew my father.”

✦ ✦ ✦

He paused.

“He would never have abandoned that notebook.”

Maya looked at the ancient object.

“So someone took it.”

“Or he hid it.”

“Why?”

Elias looked at her.

“Because he discovered something he believed could change history.”

David shook his head.

“That sounds like a conspiracy.”

Elias smiled faintly.

“Most conspiracies begin as stories people tell themselves when evidence is missing.”

“And this one?”

“This one began with evidence.”

Maya picked up one of the photographs from his satchel.

The image showed an excavation site surrounded by desert.

“Jericho?”

Elias nodded.

“My father was there in 1947.”

Maya studied the photograph.

“Who else was with him?”

“Three researchers.”

“Names?”

Elias hesitated.

Then he reached into his notebook and turned several pages.

✦ ✦ ✦

“Samuel Cohen.”

“Archaeologist?”

“Yes.”

“Who were the others?”

Elias turned another page.

“David Ben-Ari.”

Maya looked at David.

He raised an eyebrow.

“That’s a coincidence.”

Elias shook his head.

“There was also a woman.”

“Who?”

Elias stared at the faded handwriting.

“Leah.”

“Leah what?”

“That is the problem.”

The last name had been scratched out.

Maya leaned closer.

“Why would someone erase her name?”

“I don’t know.”

Elias turned another page.

There was a drawing.

A symbol.

The same symbol carved into the seal.

Maya felt a chill move through her.

✦ ✦ ✦

“This is the mark.”

“Yes.”

“What does it mean?”

Elias studied it.

“My father believed it represented a doorway.”

“A doorway to what?”

He looked at the broken tablet.

“That’s what he never wrote down.”

A sound came from outside.

Footsteps.

David turned toward the entrance.

“Are we expecting someone?”

“No,” Maya said.

The footsteps stopped.

Everyone became silent.

Elias slowly closed his notebook.

“Put the seal away.”

Maya looked at him.

“Why?”

“Because whoever took my father’s notebook may have been looking for this.”

The tent flap moved.

A shadow passed across the entrance.

David reached for the nearest flashlight.

“Who’s there?”

No answer.

✦ ✦ ✦

Maya looked at Elias.

He whispered:

“Don’t open it.”

The shadow disappeared.

Then came the sound of a vehicle starting in the distance.

David rushed outside.

Maya followed.

A cloud of dust hung over the road.

But there was no vehicle in sight.

David returned moments later.

“Whoever it was, they’re gone.”

Maya looked down at the seal.

Elias was still holding his father’s notebook.

For the first time, Maya understood that the tablet was not simply an archaeological discovery.

Someone else knew it existed.

Someone else had been waiting for it.

And now—

they knew Maya had found it.

Elias closed the notebook.

“We leave tonight.”

Maya looked at him.

“Where are we going?”

Elias met her eyes.

“Jericho.”

And beneath the ancient desert, something waited for them.

Chapter Five

Jericho

No Man's Land

They left before sunrise.

Maya sat beside Elias in the front of the vehicle while David drove.

For the first hour, none of them spoke.

The tablet was secured inside a protective case beneath Maya’s seat.

The seal was wrapped separately.

Elias’s father’s notebook rested inside his satchel.

Three objects.

Three pieces of a mystery that had survived generations.

Maya finally broke the silence.

“Why Jericho?”

Elias looked through the window.

“Because that’s where my father found the first symbol.”

“You said he was ordered to forget it.”

“Yes.”

“By whom?”

Elias was quiet.

“An organization.”

Maya waited.

“What organization?”

Elias looked at her.

“They called themselves the Keepers.”

✦ ✦ ✦

David glanced at him through the rearview mirror.

“Keepers of what?”

Elias looked down at the notebook.

“That was never explained.”

Maya studied him.

“You’ve known about them all these years?”

“I knew my father feared them.”

“That’s different.”

“I know.”

The road continued through the desert.

Eventually, the landscape began to change.

The ancient hills appeared in the distance.

Jericho.

One of the oldest continuously inhabited places on Earth.

Maya had studied its history for years.

But now she was seeing it differently.

Not as an archaeological site.

As a clue.

They arrived at an old excavation area shortly after noon.

Elias stepped out.

He stood perfectly still.

Maya noticed the expression on his face.

“You’ve been here before.”

Elias nodded.

“Once.”

“When?”

“When I was seventeen.”

David looked at him.

“You came here with your father?”

“Yes.”

“What happened?”

Elias stared toward the hills.

“My father brought me here because he wanted me to see something.”

“What?”

Elias walked toward the ruins.

“The doorway.”

Maya followed.

“There was actually a doorway?”

“Not like you think.”

They reached a section of exposed stone.

Elias knelt beside a partially buried wall.

He brushed away the dust.

A symbol appeared.

Maya froze.

It was the same symbol carved into the seal.

She looked at Elias.

“You knew it was here.”

“I suspected.”

“You brought us here because of it.”

“Yes.”

David crouched beside them.

✦ ✦ ✦

“There are no records of this structure.”

“That’s because the structure isn’t part of the official excavation.”

Maya stared at him.

“Then what is it?”

Elias brushed more dirt away.

A narrow opening appeared beneath the stones.

Maya’s pulse quickened.

“There.”

Elias nodded.

“The doorway.”

David looked at the dark opening.

“Where does it go?”

Elias took a flashlight from his bag.

“My father never found out.”

Maya looked at him.

“How do you know?”

Elias turned toward her.

“Because he told me never to open it.”

The three of them stood in silence.

Then Maya looked at the tablet.

The ancient markings seemed almost visible through the protective case.

She remembered Elias’s words.

A memory.

A doorway.

A seal.

And a name.

Yehoshua.

Maya stepped toward the opening.

Elias grabbed her arm.

“Wait.”

She turned.

“What?”

He pointed toward the stone.

There were words carved beneath the symbol.

Maya leaned closer.

For several moments, neither of them spoke.

Then Elias translated the first line.

“Those who enter…”

He stopped.

Maya looked at him.

“What does the rest say?”

Elias swallowed.

“Will remember.”

✦ ✦ ✦

The wind moved across the ruins.

Maya looked into the darkness.

Then, from somewhere beneath the earth—

came three slow knocks.

David stepped backward.

“Tell me that was the wind.”

Elias shook his head.

“It wasn’t.”

Three more knocks answered from below.

Maya looked at the doorway.

Something was on the other side.

And it knew they had arrived.

Chapter Six

The Watchers

The Eyes Beneath

The knocking stopped.

Maya stared into the darkness beneath the ruins.

Nothing moved.

No voice.

No footsteps.

Only silence.

Then Elias whispered:

“Back away from the doorway.”

Maya didn’t move.

“Professor…”

“Now.”

Something in his voice made her obey.

David stepped beside her.

“What exactly are we dealing with?”

Elias didn’t answer.

He was staring at the symbol carved into the stone.

His face had gone pale.

Maya noticed.

“You’ve seen this before.”

Elias shook his head.

“Not this one.”

“Then why are you afraid?”

✦ ✦ ✦

He looked at her.

“Because my father described what came after it.”

A cold wind moved through the ruins.

Maya felt the temperature drop.

“What did he describe?”

Elias opened his father’s notebook.

His hands were trembling.

He turned to a page near the back.

The handwriting was different there.

More hurried.

Almost frantic.

Maya leaned closer.

“What does it say?”

Elias read silently.

Then he closed the notebook.

“Tell us,” David said.

Elias looked toward the doorway.

“He wrote that the symbol was never meant to be a warning.”

Maya frowned.

“What was it?”

“A marker.”

“For what?”

Elias looked at her.

“For the ones who were watching.”

✦ ✦ ✦

A sound came from behind them.

A stone moved.

David spun around.

“Who’s there?”

Nothing.

Maya raised her flashlight.

The beam crossed the ruins.

Empty.

Then she saw something.

A footprint.

Fresh.

In the dust.

Maya’s heart began pounding.

“David.”

He followed her gaze.

Another footprint appeared several feet away.

Then another.

Leading toward them.

But there was no one there.

Elias whispered:

“They’re here.”

Maya turned toward him.

“Who?”

Before Elias could answer, every light went out.

Darkness swallowed the ruins.

David cursed.

✦ ✦ ✦

Maya heard movement.

Fast.

Something passed close enough to touch her shoulder.

She turned and swung her flashlight.

Nothing.

Then a voice came from the darkness.

“Maya.”

She froze.

The voice was barely audible.

But she knew it.

It was her own voice.

David grabbed her arm.

“Don’t answer.”

“How did it know my name?”

Elias spoke from somewhere behind them.

“Because it has been waiting.”

Maya turned toward him.

“Waiting for what?”

✦ ✦ ✦

A faint light appeared inside the doorway.

Not a flashlight.

Not fire.

Something pale and blue.

The symbol carved into the stone began to glow.

Elias stepped backward.

“No.”

Maya stared.

“What?”

He looked at the tablet case.

“It has begun.”

The tablet was glowing too.

The markings across its surface were changing.

Lines appeared where there had been none.

Symbols rearranged themselves before their eyes.

Maya stepped closer.

“Elias…”

He grabbed her arm.

“Don’t touch it.”

But Maya wasn’t looking at the tablet anymore.

She was looking into the doorway.

A figure stood on the other side.

Tall.

Motionless.

Watching them.

David raised his flashlight.

The beam struck the figure.

For one second, Maya saw a face.

Ancient.

Human.

And impossibly familiar.

Then the light went out again.

✦ ✦ ✦

The figure disappeared.

A whisper came from the darkness.

Not from the doorway.

From behind Maya.

“You were not supposed to find us.”

Maya turned.

Nothing.

Elias was staring at the ground.

There was a new inscription carved into the dust.

Four words.

Maya read them aloud.

“The first one has awakened.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Elias closed his eyes.

And somewhere beneath Jericho—

something opened its eyes.

Chapter Seven

The Voice

Maya couldn’t breathe.

The words were still visible in the dust.

THE FIRST ONE HAS AWAKENED.

David stared at them.

“First one?”

Elias said nothing.

He was looking at the doorway.

The blue light had disappeared, but something had changed.

The darkness beyond the opening no longer looked empty.

It looked deep.

Far too deep.

Maya turned toward Elias.

“You know what this means.”

“I know what my father believed it meant.”

“That’s not the same thing.”

“No.”

Elias slowly opened the old notebook.

“There are things I never told you.”

David looked at him.

“Now would be a good time.”

Elias turned several pages.

✦ ✦ ✦

Near the center of the notebook was a drawing of the same symbol they had just seen.

Beneath it were three handwritten words.

THEY ARE RETURNING.

Maya felt a chill.

“Who?”

Elias looked at her.

“The Watchers.”

The word seemed to hang in the air.

Maya shook her head.

“That’s mythology.”

“So was Troy.”

David looked toward the doorway.

“And if they’re real?”

Elias closed the notebook.

“Then we’ve already made a mistake.”

“What mistake?”

“Coming here.”

A low vibration passed through the ground.

Dust fell from the stones.

Maya stepped backward.

The vibration stopped.

Then came a sound.

A whisper.

So faint that Maya almost thought she imagined it.

“Maya…”

David looked at her.

“You heard that?”

Elias’s expression changed.

“Don’t answer.”

The voice came again.

“Maya…”

This time it was closer.

She turned toward the doorway.

“Who are you?”

Elias grabbed her shoulder.

“No!”

The ground beneath them shook violently.

A crack raced across the stone floor.

David stumbled.

The ancient doorway groaned.

Something struck the other side.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

The same rhythm as before.

Maya stared into the darkness.

Then she saw it.

A hand.

✦ ✦ ✦

Pale fingers curled around the edge of the doorway.

David raised the flashlight.

The hand vanished.

Elias whispered:

“They’ve found the entrance.”

Maya looked at him.

“Who?”

There was only one sentence.

WHEN THE FIRST ONE AWAKENS, THE OTHERS WILL FOLLOW.

“First one…”

Then at the seal.

Then back toward the doorway.

“Why me?”

Elias didn’t answer.

The tablet suddenly cracked.

A thin line of light appeared across its surface.

Maya stepped closer.

Words began forming in the clay.

Not ancient symbols this time.

English.

Three words.

HE REMEMBERS YOU.

“Who?”

A man’s voice.

Calm.

Ancient.

And unmistakably human.

“Yehoshua.”

The name echoed through the ruins.

Then every light returned.

The doorway was empty.

But on the ground before it was something that had not been there before.

The voices became silent.

Then something enormous moved beyond the mountains.

Elias raised the ancient blade.

“They’re coming.”

Maya opened her eyes.

“Who?”

✦ ✦ ✦

The first Keeper appeared on the horizon.

Behind him stood an army of Shomrim.

But they weren’t alone.

Something followed them.

Tall figures covered in black stone.

The same beings Maya had seen in the visions.

The original guardians.

Her mother whispered:

“They were never protecting humanity.”

Maya looked at her.

“What were they protecting?”

Her mother answered:

“The cycle.”

✦ ✦ ✦

The first Keeper raised his staff.

“Stop her!”

The Shomrim charged.

Elias stepped forward.

Maya grabbed his arm.

“Don’t.”

He looked at her.

“Why?”

“Because this isn’t a battle.”

“What is it?”

Maya walked toward the army.

The ground beneath her feet illuminated.

The ancient symbol appeared again.

The Shomrim stopped.

The first Keeper stared at her.

“You remember.”

“Yes.”

“You know what happens if you break the cycle.”

“Yes.”

“Then you know the cost.”

Maya looked toward the sky.

“I do.”

✦ ✦ ✦

The Keeper lowered his staff.

“What will you sacrifice?”

Maya turned toward Elias.

Then her mother.

Then the black stone.

Finally, she looked at herself.

“Everything I thought I was.”

The black stone exploded.

A wave of light swept across the world.

The Shomrim fell to their knees.

The ancient guardians disappeared.

The mountains stopped shaking.

The sky cleared.

For the first time in thousands of years—

there was silence.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elias walked toward Maya.

“Is it over?”

Maya looked toward the horizon.

“No.”

“Then what?”

She pointed toward the sky.

A single door had appeared among the stars.

It was enormous.

Ancient.

And slowly opening.

The first Keeper whispered:

“That door was never meant to open.”

Maya looked at him.

“Then why is it opening?”

The Keeper’s face went pale.

“Because something is coming through.”

✦ ✦ ✦

A shadow appeared behind the door.

Maya recognized it immediately.

The same presence from her first memory.

The one that had entered the black stone.

The one that had been waiting.

Maya whispered:

“I remember you.”

The shadow answered:

“And now I remember you.”

The door opened wider.

And the entire sky began to fall.

Chapter Thirty-Eight

The Difference

The sky was falling.

Not metaphorically.

Stars tore loose from the darkness and descended like burning fragments.

Maya stood beneath them.

She wasn’t afraid.

Not anymore.

Elias grabbed her hand.

“We need to get out of here.”

“There is nowhere to go.”

“There has to be.”

Maya looked at him.

“There isn’t.”

Her mother stepped forward.

“There is one place.”

Maya turned.

“Where?”

Her mother pointed toward the door in the sky.

“Beyond it.”

Elias shook his head.

“No.”

Maya stared at the enormous doorway.

“What is beyond the door?”

Her mother whispered:

“Everything that was forgotten.”

✦ ✦ ✦

The first Keeper approached.

His staff was broken.

The symbols across his robes were fading.

“If she enters, the boundary will collapse.”

Maya looked at him.

“Then why was the door created?”

The old man lowered his eyes.

“For you.”

Maya froze.

“What?”

“The door wasn’t built to keep something out.”

He looked toward the sky.

“It was built to let you return.”

Elias stepped forward.

“Return from where?”

The Keeper looked at Maya.

“From the place before this world.”

✦ ✦ ✦

The ground shook.

The doorway began descending.

Closer.

Closer.

Until it hung above Maya like a second moon.

A voice came through.

“Māyā.”

The sound made every stone around them vibrate.

Maya closed her eyes.

Memories flooded through her.

She remembered the first world.

The second.

The third.

The countless worlds that had come and gone.

She remembered every time humanity had forgotten.

Every time the Shomrim had rebuilt the seal.

Every time she had returned.

And she remembered the first choice.

She had created the cycle.

Not to punish humanity.

To protect it.

But somewhere along the way, the Keepers had misunderstood.

They believed memory was the danger.

Maya finally understood.

Forgetting was the danger.

✦ ✦ ✦

She opened her eyes.

“The cycle doesn’t need to continue.”

Her mother took her hand.

“What will you do?”

Maya looked at Elias.

He already knew.

“No,” he whispered.

She smiled sadly.

“I have to.”

“You don’t.”

“If I don’t go through, the door will keep opening.”

“Then close it.”

“I can’t.”

“Why?”

“Because it was never mine to close.”

✦ ✦ ✦

The doorway opened completely.

A vast light appeared beyond it.

Maya stepped forward.

Elias followed.

She stopped him.

“You can’t come.”

“I’ve followed you this far.”

“I know.”

“Then let me finish it with you.”

Maya touched his face.

“You already did.”

Her mother began crying.

“Maya…”

Maya turned toward her.

“I finally understand why you gave me away.”

Her mother shook her head.

“I was trying to save you.”

Maya smiled.

“You did.”

Maya kissed her forehead.

Then she turned toward the door.

Elias reached for her.

“Maya!”

She looked back.

For a moment, she was simply herself.

Not the first memory.

Not the chosen one.

Not the keeper of worlds.

Just Maya.

She smiled.

“Remember me.”

Then she stepped through.

The doorway vanished.

The falling stars stopped.

The world became silent.

Elias stood alone.

Her mother closed her eyes.

✦ ✦ ✦

Then—

a single star appeared above them.

It began moving.

Forming a shape.

A symbol.

Maya’s symbol.

Elias stared at it.

“She didn’t disappear.”

The first Keeper whispered:

“No.”

He looked toward the sky.

“She went home.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Maya opened her eyes.

Older.

Alive.

Crying.

Maya stared at him.

“Elias?”

He smiled through his tears.

“You remembered.”

She looked down at the child.

The baby opened its eyes.

They were not black.

They were bright.

Human.

Maya smiled.

“What do we call her?”

Elias looked at Maya.

“You already know.”

Maya looked toward the window.

The morning sun was rising.

And somewhere beyond the world—

the ancient door remained.

Waiting.

Not for Maya.

For the child.

A name that had never been forgotten.

“Eden.”

Once.

Twice.

Then stopped.

And beneath the floor, something whispered.
